Know the signs

When should you stop ignoring it?

Tap each sign to see when you should get it assessed, and what happens if you don't.

01

Elbow & Forearm Pain

Pain on the outside of the elbow when you grip or hit?

 ›

Overloaded forearm tendons are one of the most common racquet-sport problems. They rarely settle while you keep playing through them.

Consult us if...

  • Pain on the outer side of the elbow when you grip the racquet or hit a backhand
  • Lifting a cup, turning a door handle or shaking hands hurts
  • Your grip feels weaker than it used to
  • It aches at night or the day after a match, not just during play
⚠ Don't ignore it. The longer you play through it, the harder it is to settle.
02

Shoulder Pain on Overhead Shots

Smash, serve or clear starting to pinch?

 ›

Repeated overhead shots load the shoulder. A pinch or catch at the top of the swing is a signal to get it checked, not to push through.

Consult us if...

  • Pinching or pain when you lift your arm overhead or reach behind you
  • Pain when you lie on that side at night
  • Your smash or serve has lost power or feels restricted
  • You are starting to avoid high shots without meaning to
🔴 See us this week. It tends to get worse with every overhead shot.
03

Knee Pain on Lunges & Landings

Pain just below the kneecap after a long session?

 ›

Sudden stops, lunges and jump landings load the knee again and again. Front-of-knee pain that keeps coming back is worth assessing early.

Consult us if...

  • Pain just below the kneecap when you lunge, jump or land
  • Stairs, or getting up after sitting for a while, bother the knee
  • It is stiff or sore the morning after you play
  • The pain has built up over weeks rather than from one bad moment
⚠ Worth assessing early. Overload problems are easier to fix before they linger.
04

Ankle Giving Way

Rolled it again on a quick turn?

 ›

An ankle that keeps rolling usually means the joint has lost stability. It isn't just bad luck.

Consult us if...

  • Your ankle rolls on flat ground or on a quick change of direction
  • It still feels weak or unsteady weeks after a sprain
  • You tape or brace it just to be able to play
  • Swelling hasn't gone down after 48 hours
🔴 Get it checked before your next match. Repeat sprains do more damage each time.
05

Pain That Won't Settle

Still bothering you 72 hours after you played?

 ›

Pain that lingers, or changes how you move, is often your body protecting something. That is how one problem becomes two.

Consult us if...

  • Pain is still there 3 days after playing
  • You have been managing it with painkillers or heat for weeks
  • Pain wakes you up at night
  • You are moving differently or avoiding certain shots without realising it
🔴 See us. This needs a proper assessment, not more rest.

These are general signs, not a diagnosis. A proper assessment is the only way to know what is going on.
